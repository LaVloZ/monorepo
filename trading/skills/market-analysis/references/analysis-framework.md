# Analysis Framework

Use this reference when you need a fuller checklist or when the user asks for a more structured explanation.

## Quick Checklist

- What asset is being analyzed?
- What timeframe matters for the question?
- Is the asset trending, ranging, or reacting to news?
- Where are the nearest support and resistance levels?
- Is price accepting above resistance or rejecting from it?
- Is momentum expanding or fading?
- What fresh catalyst may explain the move?
- Does the catalyst affect rates, growth, regulation, margins, supply, or sentiment?
- What is the bullish scenario?
- What is the bearish scenario?
- What would invalidate the current thesis?

## Teaching Mode

When the user wants a simple explanation:

- Start with one sentence describing what price is doing.
- Explain only the 2-3 most important levels or forces.
- Use plain language like "buyers defended this zone" or "the market is pricing in slower growth."
- Avoid indicator overload unless the user explicitly asks for more detail.

## Catalyst Guide

Common drivers to check:

- Central bank decisions, minutes, and rate guidance
- CPI, PPI, jobs, GDP, retail sales, PMI, and other macro releases
- Earnings, guidance, buybacks, dilution, and capital raises
- Regulation, ETF decisions, lawsuits, bans, or approvals
- Tariffs, sanctions, war, elections, or executive decisions
- Sector rotation, risk-on/risk-off flows, and dollar moves

## Scenario Template

Use compact scenario wording:

- Bullish: If price holds above the key level and buyers defend dips, continuation toward the next resistance stays open.
- Bearish: If price loses the key level and bounces fail below it, a move toward lower support becomes more likely.
- Neutral: If price remains trapped between support and resistance, expect range behavior until a catalyst or breakout appears.
